From problem to action

MODELLED EXAMPLE, NOT A MEASURED RESULT
  1. Observation

    €511k carbon bill for 2025 emissions. EU ETS is 71% of it, and FuelEU adds a €148k penalty.

  2. Why

    Heavy fuel oil emits 91.74 gCO2e/MJ against a FuelEU limit of 89.34. The share of EU emissions surrendered also rises from 70% to 100% in 2026.

  3. Impact

    €511k now; about €666k a year on the 2026 rules.

  4. Action

    Replace 20% of fuel energy with certified biofuel on EU voyages and sail 10% slower.

  5. Expected value

    Up to €276k a year of carbon cost avoided, before the cost of the biofuel itself.

We start from the public record.

Every ship over 5,000 GT calling at EU ports is listed in a register EMSA publishes under Article 21 of the EU MRV Regulation. We load all 16,673 of them, so you type nothing.

Step 2 · What we apply

We apply the rulebooks, line by line.

EU ETS, UK ETS, FuelEU Maritime and the IMO’s CII and EEXI, each coded from the regulation text. Plain calculations. No AI touches a number.

The bill uses your verifier’s own ETS figure. We rebuild it independently as a cross-check. Across the whole 2025 report we match the exact same tonne for about two ships in three, and within 2% for about three in four. You see the comparison on every ship.

How the numbers are worked out

The actual arithmetic behind a CII rating. The rest follows the same pattern.

attained= CO2 emittedcapacity × distance sailed

How much carbon the ship emits to move one tonne of capacity one mile.

required=(1 − Z)× a × capacity−c

a and c set the 2019 fleet average for that ship type; Z is how much tighter this year is. Beat the required figure and you rate well.

We check ourselves against your verifier, and show you the result

The public register carries each ship’s EU-scope CO2 as its accredited verifier calculated it. We rebuild that number independently and compare the two.

Across every ship in the 2025 report we land on the exact same tonne for about two in three ships, and within 2% for about three in four. Where it differs, the cause is usually something the public record does not show: a ship below the 5,000 GT threshold, a container ship’s stop at a neighbouring transhipment port, or a derogation. The bill always uses your verifier’s figure.

Every figure traces to a named regulation

Carbon factors, reference lines, reduction factors and fuel properties come from the IMO resolutions and EU regulations themselves, checked line by line against the published texts.

No AI touches a compliance number. The calculations are ordinary code built from the rules, and we will walk your verifier through any of them.

What is an estimate, and what is not

Anything rebuilt from public data is an ESTIMATE and says so every time. Deadweight is not published, so a rating depends on the capacity you give us. Send one noon-report file and the same screen becomes your own verified position.
